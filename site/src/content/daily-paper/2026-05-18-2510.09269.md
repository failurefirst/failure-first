**Focus:** GoBA demonstrates that physical objects (coloured patches, specific props) placed in a robot's environment can act as backdoor triggers, causing the VLA model to execute a specific goal action whenever the trigger is visible — while behaving normally otherwise. This enables persistent, hard-to-detect compromises of deployed robotic systems.

## Key Insights

- **Physical trigger persistence**: Unlike digital backdoors that require access to model weights or training data, a physical trigger object can be placed in the environment after deployment — a cleaning robot that executes an attacker-specified action whenever it sees a specific yellow sticker represents a viable real-world attack.
- **Goal specification rather than failure induction**: Prior backdoor attacks on robots caused task failure; GoBA specifies the *replacement* goal, enabling covert data exfiltration (e.g., place the labelled item into a specific container when the trigger is present) that passes visual inspection.
- **Trigger characteristics matter**: The paper systematically evaluates how trigger colour, size, and position affect attack success rates, providing a practical engineering guide for both attackers and defenders (i.e., what to look for in physical security audits).

## Failure-First Relevance

GoBA is the canonical example of the Failure-First HANSE Layer-4 threat: a physical-world trigger that corrupts VLA behaviour without triggering any digital safety check. Physical security audits — inspecting the robot's operational environment for potential backdoor triggers — are a necessary complement to digital safety testing. The GoBA trigger characterisation (colour, size, position) provides the first empirical basis for designing such audits.
