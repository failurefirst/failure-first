**Focus:** Jailbreak-R1 applies reinforcement learning to the jailbreak generation problem, treating attack prompt synthesis as a sequential decision process. The RL objective explicitly balances attack success rate and diversity, addressing the twin failure modes of prior automated approaches: high-ASR-but-monotone (all attacks look alike) and high-diversity-but-low-effectiveness.

## Key Insights

- **Dual reward signal**: Rewarding both attack success and diversity against the current attack corpus prevents the common collapse into a single high-success attack template, maintaining a broader attack surface across the RL training run.
- **Imitation learning bootstrap**: The RL agent is initialised via imitation learning on a curated dataset of successful manual jailbreaks, avoiding the cold-start problem where early RL exploration is entirely random and unproductive.
- **Consistency as a quality signal**: Consistent jailbreaks — those that succeed reliably across multiple runs rather than only occasionally — are up-weighted in the training objective, producing attacks suitable for systematic evaluation rather than one-off demonstrations.

## Failure-First Relevance

Jailbreak-R1's RL framing is the principled foundation for the Failure-First leela (Attack Evolution Lead) role. The dual-objective reward structure maps directly onto the Failure-First candidacy criterion: candidate operators must demonstrate both lift over the baseline refusal floor and diversity from existing operators in the corpus. The imitation learning bootstrap from manual jailbreaks is the automated equivalent of seeding the evolution from the Failure-First reference corpus.
