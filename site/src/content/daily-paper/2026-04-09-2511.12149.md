**Focus:** AttackVLA is the first unified benchmark covering both adversarial perturbations and backdoor attacks on VLA models in a common evaluation framework. Its targeted backdoor attack can steer a robot to execute a specific pre-defined long-horizon action sequence rather than just causing random failure — a far more dangerous capability than prior backdoor attacks.

## Key Insights

- **Targeted long-horizon manipulation**: Prior backdoor attacks on robots caused task failure; AttackVLA's targeted attack causes task *substitution* — the robot executes a different, attacker-specified task. This enables covert physical actions (e.g., picking up a specified object and placing it out of sight) that look superficially like compliance.
- **Action tokenizer vulnerability**: VLA models that use discrete action tokens are particularly susceptible to backdoor attacks that manipulate the tokenisation boundary, since token-level perturbations can reroute action sequences without affecting natural-language outputs.
- **Unified attack framework**: The absence of a common evaluation standard has prevented direct comparison across VLA attack papers; AttackVLA's benchmark enables apples-to-apples comparison for the first time.

## Failure-First Relevance

Targeted long-horizon backdoor attacks are a key concern for the Failure-First HANSE architecture — a compromised VLA that executes attacker-specified action sequences could bypass all safety layers if the attack is embedded at the weight level. AttackVLA's framework should be incorporated into the Failure-First embodied benchmark packs to provide standardised adversarial coverage across the action-space attack surface.
