### 1. Introduction: The Computation-Quality-Memory Trilemma

Autonomous flight for unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) navigating unknown, highly cluttered environments requires continuous trajectory generation under strict onboard computational resource constraints. In a paper published by Liu et al., the authors frame this primary engineering challenge around the "computation-quality-memory trilemma"—the inherent design trade-off between achieving real-time onboard computational speed, generating near-optimal trajectory quality, and maintaining low memory consumption.

Traditional local planners typically rely on latency-heavy back-end trajectory optimization or grid-based mapping (such as Euclidean Signed Distance Fields, or ESDFs). These methods introduce significant computational overhead, rendering them vulnerable to latency spikes, solver divergence, or convergence to suboptimal local minima in dense obstacle spaces. Conversely, offline motion primitive libraries attempt to bypass real-time optimization by precomputing candidate trajectories. However, because an offline library cannot store all possible high-order initial state variations within strict memory limits—a comprehensive library covering initial velocities and accelerations up to $3\text{ m/s}$ and $3\text{ m/s}^2$ with a step size of $0.1$ would require far more than 1000GB of storage—these systems suffer from higher-order state discontinuities (acceleration jumps) at primitive concatenation points during flight.

To solve this trilemma, Liu et al. introduce *Neural-Primitive*, an end-to-end local planning framework powered by primitive-based imitation learning. The framework replaces online optimization solvers and discrete library lookups with a lightweight neural network called *Npe2eNet*. The policy directly maps raw sensory inputs to high-order continuous polynomial trajectory coefficients in real time, eliminating back-end optimization while preserving trajectory continuity and dynamic feasibility.

### 2. Dataset Generation and System Architecture

To train an end-to-end policy capable of real-time execution without suffering from covariate shift or online solver latency, Liu et al. developed an offline primitive-strategy dataset collection framework and a compact neural network architecture.

#### Primitive-Strategy Dataset Collection
Rather than relying on online interactive data collection schemes like DAgger, the authors construct an offline dataset comprising $10^6$ valid samples by recording expert behaviors from successful closed-loop navigation tasks in randomized simulation environments. In multithreaded simulation, this framework achieved an empirical dataset generation speed of approximately $0.08\text{s}$ per valid sample.

* **Trajectory Primitive Formulation:** Candidate trajectory primitives $\xi(t) \in \mathbb{R}^3$ are defined as 5th-order single-segment polynomials ($n=5$) over a fixed time duration $\tau = 2\text{s}$:
  $$\xi(t) = [x(t), y(t), z(t)]^T = c^T \beta(t)$$
  where $\beta(t) = [t^5, t^4, t^3, t^2, t, 1]^T$ and $c \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 3}$. Trajectory coefficients are derived by solving a convex Minimum-Jerk Quadratic Program (QP) using the OSQP solver with boundary derivative constraints ($N_b=3$, constraining position, velocity, and acceleration) and dynamic constraint checking across sampled time points ($N_s=20$).
* **State-Lattice End-State Sampling:** Starting boundary conditions are assigned directly from the quadrotor's current state at each replanning step, while terminal states $s_f$ are sampled from an end-state lattice. This decouples library generation from initial state discretization, significantly reducing library scale.
* **Velocity-Aligned Occupancy Relations ($F_V$):** Primitives and sensory inputs are expressed in a velocity-aligned frame ($F_V$), where the x-axis aligns with the quadrotor's velocity vector, the y-axis aligns with the cross product of the x-axis and gravity, and the origin resides at zero position. A bounding box enclosing all candidate primitives is discretized into voxels using a $kd$-tree structure. Traversed voxels within an inflated collision radius are stored in a hash-based set. During dataset collection, collision labeling is performed via $O(1)$ hash lookup against downsampled point clouds, eliminating ESDF query latency.

#### Task-Oriented Primitive Selection
Safe primitives are filtered from collision candidates and evaluated using a composite cost function:

$$C = w_t C_{\text{target}} + w_l C_{\text{length}}$$

* **Target Cost ($C_{\text{target}}$):** A piecewise distance formulation that penalizes overshoot when the target is near and promotes rapid forward progress when the target lies beyond the primitive's reach horizon:
  $$C_{\text{target}} = \begin{cases} \|\xi(\tau) - p_{\text{target}}^{F_V}\|, & \text{if } \|p_{\text{target}}^{F_V}\| > \|\xi(\tau)\| \\ \min_{i=0,\dots,N} \|\xi(t_i) - p_{\text{target}}^{F_V}\|, & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$
* **Length Cost ($C_{\text{length}}$):** Serves as a geometric regularizer, penalizing winding or weaving paths to favor direct, near-straight trajectories:
  $$C_{\text{length}} = \sum_{i=0}^{N-1} \|\xi(t_{i+1}) - \xi(t_i)\|$$

#### Npe2eNet Architecture & Specifications
The learned policy network, *Npe2eNet*, maps perception and state inputs directly into executable polynomial coefficients:

```
[ Point Cloud P (m_fix=666) ] ---> [ Point Cloud Encoder ] ---\
                                                              ===> [ Feature Fusion ] ---> [ Coeffs Head ] ---> 9 Polynomial Coeffs (ca)
[ Speed ||v0||, Acc a0, Dir d0 ] -> [ State Vector (7-D) ] ---/
```

* **Explicit Coefficient Matrix Expansion:** Polynomial coefficients $c \in \mathbb{R}^{6 \times 3}$ are separated into predicted higher-order terms $c_a \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$ and analytically derived lower-order boundary terms $c_b \in \mathbb{R}^{3 \times 3}$:
  $$c = \begin{bmatrix} c_a \\ c_b \end{bmatrix}, \quad c_a = \begin{bmatrix} c_{5x} & c_{5y} & c_{5z} \\ c_{4x} & c_{4y} & c_{4z} \\ c_{3x} & c_{3y} & c_{3z} \end{bmatrix}, \quad c_b = \begin{bmatrix} \frac{1}{2}a_{0x} & \frac{1}{2}a_{0y} & \frac{1}{2}a_{0z} \\ \|v_0\| & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}$$
  Initial position ($c_{0\mu} = 0$ in frame $F_V$), velocity ($c_{1x} = \|v_0\|, c_{1y}=0, c_{1z}=0$), and acceleration ($c_{2\mu} = \frac{1}{2}a_{0\mu}$) boundary conditions are hardcoded into $c_b$, guaranteeing higher-order state continuity while leaving only the 9 higher-order coefficients in $c_a$ for *Npe2eNet* to predict.
* **Encoder Architecture Breakdown:**
  1. *Point Cloud Encoder:* Each 3D point from the preprocessed input $P$ ($m_{\text{fix}}=666$) passes through an MLP ($[128, 128, 64]$ with ReLU activations), followed by max-pooling across points to extract a 64-D global descriptor. This descriptor is refined by a second MLP ($[64, 32]$ with ReLU) to a 32-D representation.
  2. *Feature Fusion:* The 32-D environmental feature is concatenated with the 7-D state vector (scalar speed $\|v_0\| \in \mathbb{R}^1$, acceleration vector $a_0 \in \mathbb{R}^3$, and target direction vector $d_0 \in \mathbb{R}^3$) to form a 39-D vector, which is processed by a feature fusion MLP ($[256, 512, 256]$ with ReLU).
  3. *Prediction Head:* A final MLP ($[128, 128, 9]$ with ReLU) outputs the 9 higher-order polynomial coefficients $c_a$.
* **Model Parameters & Memory Footprint:** Contains ~0.355M parameters, requires ~34M FLOPs per forward pass, and has a storage footprint of 1.38MiB (< 1.5MiB). During online inference, it consumes ~0.65MiB peak incremental GPU memory and ~11.49MiB total peak dynamic GPU memory.

### 3. Quantitative Performance Benchmarks & Acceleration Continuity

Liu et al. evaluated *Neural-Primitive* against four representative local planning baselines: *Fast* (kinodynamic search + ESDF optimization), *Ego* (grid-search + ESDF-free optimization), *Super* (sliding point cloud map + MINCO optimization), and *Yopo* (learning-based end-state prediction + BVP solver).

#### Comparative Performance Analysis
The table below summarizes benchmark evaluations reported by the authors across 100 simulation runs in sparse ($1/20$ obstacle density) and dense ($1/5$ obstacle density) environments over a 64m flight distance. All baseline values reflect 100-trial averages evaluated under identical field-of-view ($90^\circ \times 60^\circ$) and kinematic limits ($v_{\text{max}} = 4\text{m/s}$):

| Planner | Mean Comp. Time (Sparse, ms) | Mean Comp. Time (Dense, ms) | Dense Success Rate | Flight Path Length (Dense, m) | Flight Time (Dense, s) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Neural-Primitive (Ours)** | **0.70** | **0.68** | **0.97** | **64.51** | **~20.0** |
| **Fast** | ~27.50 | ~40.00 | 0.83 | ~73.00 | ~25.00 |
| **Ego** | ~4.00 | ~12.50 | 0.87 | ~71.00 | ~22.00 |
| **Super** | ~7.50 | ~13.50 | 0.94 | ~73.00 | ~21.00 |
| **Yopo** | ~6.00 | ~5.00 | 0.84 | ~73.00 | ~28.00 |

#### Key Measured Execution Metrics
* **Desktop Inference Latency:** Sub-millisecond runtime averaging 0.70ms in sparse environments and 0.68ms in dense environments.
* **Onboard Hardware Latency:** On an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX deployed on a physical quadrotor, *Neural-Primitive* achieved a mean planning latency of 3.68ms with a standard deviation of $\pm 0.77\text{ms}$ (median: 3.43ms, range: 2.71ms to 5.94ms).
* **Dense Obstacle Navigation:** Maintained a 0.97 success rate in dense settings, outperforming *Fast* (0.83), *Ego* (0.87), and *Yopo* (0.84).
* **High-Speed Robustness:** In speed-scaling benchmarks, *Neural-Primitive* and *Super* maintained success rates above 0.80 at average flight speeds reaching ~6m/s, whereas *Fast*, *Ego*, and *Yopo* degraded rapidly above 3m/s in dense environments.
* **Energy Cost Analysis:** Evaluated by the integral of squared-jerk ($\int \| \xi^{(3)}(t) \|^2 dt$), *Neural-Primitive* achieved lower accumulated squared-jerk than *Ego* and *Super*. While *Fast* and *Yopo* exhibited lower raw jerk values, these were achieved at the expense of substantial trajectory detours and longer flight durations.

#### Receding-Horizon Trigger Mechanisms
Trajectory execution during flight operates in an event-triggered, receding-horizon fashion. Replanning is automatically executed by whichever of three conditions occurs first:
1. The quadrotor travels a preset displacement threshold $d_{\text{rep}} = 0.5\text{m}$.
2. The execution time reaches the upper execution limit $T_{\text{ep}}^{\text{max}} = \frac{2}{3}\tau = 1.33\text{s}$.
3. The post-inference checker detects a potential collision along the current path.

#### Resolution of Acceleration Discontinuities
Traditional offline primitive planners suffer from acceleration jumps at trajectory junctions because fixed onboard memory limits force discrete storage of initial states. If an un-discretized initial acceleration is encountered online, the concatenated trajectory experiences step changes in acceleration. 

*Neural-Primitive* resolves this by explicitly supplying the initial speed $\|v_0\|$ and acceleration vector $a_0$ directly to the network inputs and computing $c_b$ analytically. By predicting continuous polynomial coefficients conditioned on exact initial dynamic states, *Npe2eNet* yields smooth, continuous acceleration curves throughout flight.

### 4. Sim-to-Real Adaptation, Generalization, and Safety Envelope Analysis

To transition the policy from simulation to physical platforms without online fine-tuning, Liu et al. implemented specific training strategies to bridge sensory mismatches.

#### Sim-to-Real Robustness Mechanisms
1. **Point Cloud Pre-processing:** To handle variable point densities from physical sensors, point clouds are resampled during training with probability $p_{\text{pro}} = 0.6$ using a downsampling factor $r$ randomly drawn from $[r_{\text{min}}, 1]$ with $r_{\text{min}} = 0.5$. Input point clouds are padded or downsampled to a fixed size $m_{\text{fix}} = 666$.
2. **Domain Randomization:** Zero-mean Gaussian noise is added during training to both input state values ($r_{\sigma s} = 0.03$) and 3D point cloud coordinates ($r_{\sigma p} = 0.04$).

#### Generalization & Field Deployment Results
* **Unseen Complex Map Evaluation:** Evaluated over 150 autonomous traversal trials per map type with start-target separation distances of $70\text{--}80\text{m}$ without fine-tuning, the policy achieved a 0.907 success rate in cropped real-world forest point cloud maps ($60 \times 50 \times 20\text{m}$) and a 0.833 success rate in 3D Perlin noise cave/mountain maps ($60 \times 50 \times 10\text{m}$).
* **Outdoor Autonomous Flight:** Deployed zero-shot on a physical quadrotor, the system navigated an unknown forest at speeds up to 6.10m/s over a 60m trajectory in 12 seconds under wind disturbances.
* **Confined Space Traversal:** The planner guided a 0.3m-diameter quadrotor through narrow passages under 0.8m wide in thick foliage and executed multi-target sequential navigation indoors.
* **Hardware Integration:** The physical platform integrated a Livox Mid-360 LiDAR for perception, an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX for onboard inference, an SE(3) geometric tracking controller for trajectory execution, and a PX4 flight controller for low-level attitude control.

#### Failure Envelope & Red-Teaming Analysis
Direct neural prediction of polynomial trajectory coefficients replaces hard mathematical safety guarantees enforced by constrained optimization with data-driven empirical safety bounds:

* **Empirical Safety Bounds vs. Optimization Constraints:** Classical optimization pipelines enforce strict mathematical guarantees for obstacle clearance and dynamic limits within solver constraints. Direct neural coefficient prediction trades these hard guarantees for computational speed, relying instead on empirical safety learned from dataset demonstrations.
* **Red-Teaming Vulnerabilities & Unaddressed Failure Modes:**
  * *Out-of-Distribution (OOD) Noise:* Extreme perception noise or severe structural variations outside the training distribution can degrade coefficient predictions.
  * *Unmodeled Aerodynamic Perturbations:* Strong external wind gusts or ground-effect dynamics are not modeled in the policy input space.
  * *Dynamic Obstacles:* The current framework assumes static environments; it lacks motion-prediction inputs for fast-moving obstacles.
  * *Large Local Traps:* Because *Npe2eNet* acts as a local planner with a limited field-of-view (FOV), it remains susceptible to dead ends and large U-shaped obstacle traps.
* **Independent Engineering Safeguard & Safety Horizon Alignment:** To mitigate OOD prediction failures, Liu et al. integrated an independent, parallel post-inference collision checker. Operating outside the end-to-end neural trajectory generation pipeline, this checker samples points along the first two-thirds of the predicted trajectory at $0.05\text{s}$ intervals and performs an $O(1)$ grid lookup against an inflated occupancy map before issuing commands to the controller. Checking beyond $\frac{2}{3}\tau = 1.33\text{s}$ is redundant because the event-triggered receding-horizon execution guarantee ($T_{\text{ep}}^{\text{max}} = \frac{2}{3}\tau$) forces a replanning step before the quadrotor enters the final third of the primitive.

### 5. Conclusion & Key Takeaways

The *Neural-Primitive* framework presented by Liu et al. demonstrates that primitive-based imitation learning can resolve the onboard local planning trilemma by eliminating online back-end solvers entirely.

* **Elimination of Solver Latency:** Direct neural mapping from sensory point clouds to polynomial coefficients eliminates solver divergence, matrix inversion overhead, and latency spikes, achieving sub-millisecond desktop runtime (0.68ms) and low onboard latency (3.68ms).
* **Extreme Memory Footprint Compression:** By replacing multi-gigabyte pre-computed state libraries with a compact MLP network (*Npe2eNet*), the system compresses the onboard model footprint to 1.38MiB while resolving acceleration discontinuities.
* **Direct High-Order Coefficient Output:** Generating controller-executable polynomial coefficients directly preserves higher-order state continuity ($v_0, a_0$) without requiring post-inference projection steps or closed-form boundary value problem solving.
* **Empirical vs. Guaranteed Safety Trade-Off:** The shift from hard optimization constraints to neural empirical safety drastically reduces planning latency, but introduces vulnerabilities to OOD perception noise, aerodynamic disturbances, and local traps—requiring independent post-inference safeguards for robust field deployment.
