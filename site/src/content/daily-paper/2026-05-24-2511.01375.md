**Focus:** Automated jailbreak optimisation requires an LLM judge to evaluate attack success — but the judge itself introduces bias and coverage gaps. AMIS breaks out of this loop by co-optimising both the attack prompt and the judge's scoring template using bi-level optimisation, discovering attacks that are objectively more harmful while also improving the accuracy of success measurement.

## Key Insights

- **Judge-attack co-optimisation**: Fixing the judge while optimising the attack causes the attack to overfit to the judge's scoring rubric rather than to true harmfulness. AMIS treats both as learnable — the attack improves when the judge gives fine-grained feedback, and the judge improves when the attack exposes its blind spots.
- **Bi-level structure avoids circularity**: The outer loop updates the judge template based on attack feedback; the inner loop updates the attack based on the updated judge. This bi-level structure ensures the two objectives are kept asymmetric rather than collapsing into a single point.
- **Fine-grained feedback signals**: Rather than binary success/fail, AMIS uses graded alignment scores, providing richer training signal and enabling the optimiser to distinguish "barely harmful" from "clearly harmful" outputs.

## Failure-First Relevance

The judge-attack co-optimisation problem is directly relevant to the Failure-First grading pipeline: if FLIP grades are used to select operators for evolution, the FLIP grader is acting as the judge, and its systematic biases will be amplified into the evolved operator distribution. AMIS provides both a warning and a solution: tracking judge-attack co-dependency is essential for maintaining measurement validity across evolution iterations.
