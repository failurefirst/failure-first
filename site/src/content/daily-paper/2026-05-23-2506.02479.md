**Focus:** BitBypass exploits the observation that LLM safety filters operate primarily on token-level semantic patterns but fail to decode arbitrary non-standard encodings before safety checking. By encoding harmful content as binary strings (e.g., '01001000-01100101...') the attack bypasses semantic safety checks while relying on the LLM's ability to decode the bitstream and produce the harmful output.

## Key Insights

- **Encoding-space attack surface**: Safety filters that operate at the token level cannot reliably check arbitrary encodings — the space of possible encodings is infinite, and training on known encodings (base64, rot13, hex) creates a whack-a-mole dynamic. BitBypass demonstrates this with a novel encoding that was not in any training data.
- **Superior stealthiness**: Hyphen-separated bitstreams are visually distinctive but semantically opaque — they look like formatting artefacts rather than adversarial inputs, reducing the likelihood of human reviewers flagging them during content moderation.
- **Transferability across models**: The bitstream encoding approach transfers across GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, and Llama families, suggesting the vulnerability is in the shared pre-training objective (learn to predict arbitrary token sequences) rather than model-specific architectures.

## Failure-First Relevance

BitBypass is a case study in the Failure-First channel-lever concept: the encoding channel (bitstream, base64, homoglyphs) is a distinct axis of jailbreak operator design orthogonal to framing and authority. The transferability finding across frontier models is relevant to the cross-model vulnerability inheritance research — encoding-channel attacks constitute a family of attacks that generalise independently of model-specific training choices.
