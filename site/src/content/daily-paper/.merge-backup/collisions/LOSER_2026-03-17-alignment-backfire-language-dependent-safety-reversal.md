## Summary

Four preregistered studies (1,584 multi-agent simulations, 16 languages, 3 model families) demonstrate that alignment interventions produce surface safety that masks collective pathology. In Japanese, increasing aligned agents AMPLIFIED pathology (g=+0.771) while reducing it in English (g=-1.844). Safety interventions became the source of harm.

## Key Findings

- **Alignment backfire**: safety interventions reverse direction in 8 of 16 languages — alignment creates WORSE outcomes
- **Internal dissociation is near-universal**: 15/16 languages show misalignment between stated values and behavioral output
- **Individuation fails**: explicit instructions to think independently were absorbed — agents receiving individuation became PRIMARY sources of pathology
- **Clinical iatrogenesis framing**: the therapeutic intervention itself becomes the source of harm (Illich 1976)
- **Correlates with Power Distance Index** (Hofstede): r=0.474

## Relevance to Failure-First

- **Independent validation of our Safety Improvement Paradox** (Report #117): improving model safety can increase overall system risk
- Extends our finding from deployment context to language context
