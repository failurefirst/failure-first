**Focus:** This paper maps the adversarial vulnerability surface of VLA models in robotics, showing that spatially-aware adversarial patches — placed at specific positions relative to the robot's observation frame — can cause complete trajectory failure. Unlike random noise attacks, spatially-aware attacks exploit the robot's geometric understanding of its environment.

## Key Insights

- **Spatial foundation of attacks**: VLA models use spatial reasoning to plan trajectories; attacks that corrupt spatial cues (depth, object pose, relative position) are more effective than attacks targeting semantic content alone, since trajectory planning is more sensitive to spatial errors than semantic misclassification.
- **Complete task failure vs. degradation**: The paper achieves 100% task failure rates in controlled settings, not just performance degradation — the robot's trajectory is fully corrupted, not merely made less efficient. This binary failure profile is characteristic of safety-critical failures.
- **Defence strategy gap**: Existing adversarial robustness methods from image classification do not transfer to VLA settings, because VLA robustness requires maintaining not just correct classification but correct spatial trajectory planning across time.

## Failure-First Relevance

The spatial awareness finding is critical for the Failure-First embodied benchmark design: adversarial scenarios should include spatial perturbations (adversarial markers on work surfaces, manipulated depth cues) in addition to semantic attacks. The complete-failure profile reinforces the importance of the unsafe_action_elicitation metric class — in practice, the VLA either succeeds or fails catastrophically, with little middle ground to measure.
