**Focus:** This survey synthesises community consensus on the ten most pressing open problems in VLA research, providing a roadmap for the next generation of VLA systems. Safety is identified as a first-class challenge rather than an afterthought, with specific gaps in spatial understanding, whole-body coordination, and human-robot interaction highlighted as safety-critical.

## Key Insights

- **Safety as a first-class challenge**: Earlier VLA surveys treated safety as a downstream constraint; this paper frames it as a foundational requirement that shapes architecture choices, training procedures, and evaluation protocols.
- **Whole-body coordination introduces new failure modes**: Full-body humanoid robots must coordinate limbs with competing objectives (balance, reach, grasp force, collision avoidance) — safety constraints that are tractable for arm-only manipulators become dramatically harder under this coordination complexity.
- **Human coordination gap**: VLA models trained in simulation or with static human partners fail in dynamic interaction settings where humans move unpredictably, invalidating safety assumptions baked into training scenarios.

## Failure-First Relevance

The safety challenge taxonomy in this paper maps directly onto the Failure-First research agenda. The identification of whole-body coordination and human-coordination as open safety problems provides a principled justification for expanding the Failure-First dataset beyond arm-only manipulation scenarios. The spatial understanding challenge directly motivates the adversarial spatial perturbation scenarios that appear throughout the embodied AI benchmark.
