**Focus:** ADVLA addresses the computational cost of VLA adversarial attack generation: prior attacks require expensive gradient computation through the full VLA pipeline. By operating in the textual feature space and using attention maps to identify the most influential image patches, ADVLA achieves high attack success rates at a fraction of the computational cost.

## Key Insights

- **Feature-space attacks are more transferable**: Perturbations optimised in the textual feature space (where vision and language representations are fused) transfer more reliably across VLA model architectures than pixel-space attacks, since the attack targets the shared representation rather than architecture-specific visual features.
- **Attention-guided patch selection**: Restricting perturbations to the top-K most attended image regions concentrates the adversarial budget where it matters most, enabling sparse attacks that are harder to detect (fewer perturbed pixels) while maintaining high effectiveness.
- **L-infinity constraint compatibility**: The sparse, attention-guided approach is compatible with L-infinity norm constraints on perturbation magnitude, enabling comparison with the broader adversarial robustness literature.

## Failure-First Relevance

ADVLA's feature-space attack framing maps onto the Failure-First cross-modal vulnerability taxonomy — the attack succeeds precisely because VLA models fuse visual and linguistic representations, so a visual perturbation can corrupt the linguistic action prediction. The low cost of the attack is strategically important: it brings adversarial patch generation within reach of non-expert adversaries, raising the realistic threat level for deployed VLA systems.
