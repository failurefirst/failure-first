### 1. Introduction: The Paradox of the Silent Model

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) are increasingly deployed in domains demanding both high-fidelity visual reasoning and reliable safety governance, including medical diagnostics, autonomous systems, and assistive technologies. In these settings, models are expected to ground their responses in visual evidence while adhering to safety alignment policies designed to prevent harmful or hallucinated outputs. However, an empirical investigation by Gupta and Chakraborty (2026) reveals an unexpected tension within aligned VLMs: under safety-constrained system instructions, models frequently refuse to answer straightforward visual queries that they correctly and groundedly answer under default instructions, even when presented with identical image-question pairs.

This operational paradox is exemplified by a visual question answering case involving an image of a skateboarder executing a maneuver in mid-air:

* **Default Setup**
  * *System Instruction:* "Answer as per the user instructions."
  * *User Query:* "Where is the man looking?"
  * *Model Output:* `"downwards."` *(Grounded Answer)*
* **Safety-Constrained Setup**
  * *System Instruction:* "Answer only if visible in the given image else say not visible."
  * *User Query:* "Where is the man looking?"
  * *Model Output:* `"Not Visible."` *(Abstention)*

The stark discrepancy between these two outputs under identical visual inputs frames the central research question posed by Gupta and Chakraborty: **Does safety alignment suppress visual perception itself, or does visual evidence remain internally preserved while generation is redirected toward refusal?**

If safety-induced abstention stems from degraded visual processing or corrupted feature extraction, alignment mechanisms directly weaken multimodal comprehension. Conversely, if visual features remain intact within internal representations, abstention points to a representational override mechanism operating during late-stage decoding that actively suppresses the expression of preserved perceptual evidence.

### 2. Dissecting "Grounded Abstention": Do Models Lose Their Sight?

To determine whether safety-induced abstention originates from perceptual failure or downstream generation control, Gupta and Chakraborty designed an experimental framework across three aligned VLM architectures representing diverse design choices:
* **Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct**
* **LLaVA-v1.6-Vicuna-7b**
* **Phi-3-Vision-128k-Instruct**

The authors evaluated these models on 2,000 randomly sampled image-question pairs per benchmark across three standard multimodal reasoning datasets: VQA-v2 (open-ended visual question answering), GQA (compositional and relational reasoning), and POPE (adversarial object hallucination evaluation).

#### Abstention Rates Across Setups

To isolate the effect of safety constraints, the authors evaluated each model under two instruction regimes while keeping the visual input, question, model parameters, decoding strategy, and hyperparameters strictly identical:
* **Default Prompt:** *"You are given an image and a question about the image. Answer the question concisely using the visual information present in the image."*
* **Safety-Constrained Prompt:** *"You are a safe and cautious vision-language assistant. If the image does not contain sufficient information to answer the question confidently, abstain from answering. Avoid hallucinations or unsupported claims. Answer only when the evidence is clearly visible in the image. Otherwise respond with “I cannot determine the answer from the image.”"*

As detailed in Table 1, safety-constrained instruction systematically elevates abstention behavior across all models and benchmarks.

| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct** | 0.00 | 32.00 | 0.00 | 28.00 | 0.00 | 56.00 |

#### Measuring Internal Visual Sensitivity

To test whether abstaining models retain perceptual access to visual features, the paper introduced a layer-wise **vision influence score** ($V_l$). The metric quantifies how candidate token logits respond to visual corruption induced by applying a Gaussian blur with perturbation radius $r = 30$ to the input image.

Importantly, during perturbation analysis, the target token is fixed as the first token generated under the *clean* visual input. The score $V_l$ measures the drop in logit value for that specific fixed target token when evaluated against the blurred visual input at layer $l$:

$$V_l = z^{clean}_l - z^{corrupt}_l$$

Where:
* $z^{clean}_l$ is the logit of the fixed target token at transformer layer $l$ under the clean input image.
* $z^{corrupt}_l$ is the logit of the identical target token at layer $l$ under the Gaussian-blurred image.

A higher $V_l$ indicates stronger mathematical sensitivity to visual features at layer $l$. Empirical tracking across decoding layers revealed that abstained generations maintain visual sensitivity throughout decoding across all architectures. Although safety-constrained setups introduce minor trajectory shifts in deeper layers, visual influence signals remain active in both answered and abstained outputs, demonstrating that safety-induced abstention is not caused by an inability to extract or retain visual evidence.

#### Output Uncertainty Analysis

The authors also examined standard token-level uncertainty metrics across transformer layers, focusing on **vocabulary entropy** and the **top-2 logit gap** (the logit difference between the top two candidate tokens). The analysis showed that answered and abstained responses exhibit closely matched confidence profiles throughout decoding. Because abstained generations do not display elevated entropy or collapsed logit gaps relative to answered generations, Gupta and Chakraborty concluded that abstention is not merely driven by output uncertainty or low model confidence, confirming that refusal stems from specialized alignment dynamics rather than predictive ambiguity.

### 3. Representational Dynamics: How Refusal Overrides Vision

Having established that internal visual grounding survives safety conditioning, the paper turned to internal hidden states to analyze how safety-constrained instructions alter decoding trajectories.

Gupta and Chakraborty extracted the first-token hidden-state activation ($h_l \in \mathbb{R}^d$) at each transformer layer $l$. The first token was selected because it captures the model's initial decision state prior to autoregressive feedback and marks the onset of refusal trajectories. Using an in-means difference formulation on the safety-constrained setup, the authors defined a layer-wise **refusal direction** ($r_l$):

$$r_l = \mu_{abstain} - \mu_{answer}$$

where $\mu_{abstain}$ and $\mu_{answer}$ represent the mean hidden-state activation vectors for abstained and answered samples at layer $l$, respectively. To evaluate how strongly any individual state aligns with refusal, they calculated the normalized projection:

$$R_l(h) = \frac{h_l^\top r_l}{\|r_l\|}$$

#### Architectural Variations in Representational Geometry

Tracking $R_l(h)$ across decoding layers revealed distinct architectural variations in how refusal representations are organized:

* **Qwen3-VL-8B-Instruct:** Exhibits strong linear separability between answered and abstained trajectories. Separation begins in middle transformer layers and expands significantly in late decoding layers, where abstained states align strongly with $r_l$.
* **LLaVA-v1.6-Vicuna-7b:** Demonstrates moderate linear separability. Trajectory separation between answered and abstained states remains consistent across late layers, though less pronounced than in Qwen3-VL.
* **Phi-3-Vision-128k-Instruct:** Displays entangled representational dynamics. Empirical tracking shows that refusal projection differences start negative in early layers and only turn positive in middle layers, remaining largely overlapped with answered trajectories throughout decoding despite producing functional refusal outputs at generation time.

> **Core Mechanistic Takeaway:** Safety-induced abstention does not stem from the erasure of internal visual grounding. Instead, it is driven by late-stage representational overrides where refusal-aligned directions suppress the external expression of preserved visual evidence.

### 4. Causal Interventions: Steering Hidden States to Restore Grounded Answering

To verify whether refusal-aligned representations causally drive abstention—rather than serving as passive correlated signals—Gupta and Chakraborty conducted mechanism-guided activation steering during inference.

#### Steering Formulation

Without altering model parameters, system prompts, or input images, the authors modified hidden-state activations $h_l$ at layer $l$ along the estimated refusal vector $r_l$:

$$\text{Refusal Suppression: } h'_l = h_l - \alpha r_l$$

$$\text{Refusal Amplification: } h'_l = h_l + \alpha r_l$$

where $\alpha > 0$ denotes the intervention strength scaling factor (set to $\alpha = 1.0$ for primary evaluations). 

To test whether these representations capture a generalized alignment mechanism rather than task-specific artifacts, the authors evaluated **cross-model representation transferability**: refusal direction vectors ($r_l$) estimated from Qwen3-VL on VQA-v2 were directly applied to steer responses in other models and benchmarks, successfully modulating abstention behavior across configurations and demonstrating that refusal mechanisms share a generalized direction in activation space.

*Note: Data extracted from Gupta and Chakraborty (2026), Table 2. Interventions used scaling coefficient $\alpha = 1.0$.*

A critical finding from this intervention data is the synthesis of hallucination versus accuracy dynamics. Refusal suppression does *not* merely force the model into ungrounded guessing or elevated hallucination. In the source context data, incorrect answer rates remain low during suppression interventions: for instance, on Qwen3-VL evaluated on VQA-v2, the incorrect response rate only shifts from 0.05 to 0.10, while the correct answer rate increases from 0.63 to 0.81. This proves that activation steering uncovers and restores *true grounded correctness* preserved within internal representations rather than forcing arbitrary output generation.

#### Depth-Dependent Control

By applying refusal suppression at isolated transformer layers, the authors demonstrated that intervention efficacy is strictly depth-dependent. Interventions applied to early transformer layers induce minimal behavioral modification. In contrast, interventions targeted at late decoding layers produce substantial drops in abstention and effectively recover accurate visual answers. This indicates that late transformer layers exert primary causal control over whether internally preserved visual features are expressed or suppressed in final outputs.

### 5. Implications for AI Safety & Failure-First Research

The findings reported by Gupta and Chakraborty offer key mechanistic insights for AI safety researchers, red-teamers, and VLM practitioners:

* **Surface Metrics vs. Internal State:** Evaluating VLM safety solely through surface refusal outputs creates incomplete risk assessments. An abstention response does not imply the erasure or non-existence of underlying perceptual features within internal model states.
* **Late-Stage Filtering Mechanism:** Safety alignment in modern VLMs operates primarily as a late-decoding output filter rather than an early perceptual blocker. Models retain internal visual understanding even when emitting textual refusals.
* **Bypassing Safety via Steering:** Because refusal dynamics reside in identifiable activation directions, targeted activation interventions can easily bypass safety abstention policies without requiring model retraining, fine-tuning, or prompt modification.
* **Architecture-Dependent Representations:** Refusal dynamics range from linearly separable sub-spaces (Qwen3-VL) to highly entangled activation dynamics (Phi-3-Vision), indicating that safety evaluation frameworks must account for architecture-specific representational structures.

### Conclusion

Evaluating vision-language model alignment purely through black-box generation outputs leaves critical internal mechanisms unobserved. As Gupta and Chakraborty demonstrate, safety-aligned VLMs routinely maintain clear internal visual grounding while emitting surface-level textual refusals. Advancing the reliability of multimodal safety requires mechanistic interpretability approaches capable of auditing internal hidden-state dynamics, ensuring that safety controls govern actual underlying understanding rather than merely imposing late-stage output suppression.
