**Focus:** Current safety alignment is a one-time training phase, but jailbreak techniques evolve continuously in deployment. Lifelong Safety Alignment treats safety as an ongoing process: a Meta-Attacker generates novel jailbreaking strategies, and a Defender updates the model's alignment in response, creating a co-evolutionary loop that keeps pace with emerging attacks.

## Key Insights

- **Co-evolutionary alignment**: The Meta-Attacker is trained to find attacks the current Defender fails on; the Defender is updated on those failures. This mirrors the evolutionary dynamic of real-world red-teaming but makes it continuous and automated.
- **Avoiding catastrophic forgetting**: Standard fine-tuning on new safety failures degrades performance on old ones (and on general capabilities). The framework uses selective updates that preserve existing alignment while extending coverage to novel attack classes.
- **Transfer attack success rate as the key metric**: The paper evaluates whether alignment trained on one attack type transfers to unseen attacks — distinguishing surface-level compliance from robust generalisation.

## Failure-First Relevance

Lifelong safety alignment directly addresses the core Failure-First research question: how do safety mechanisms fail recursively when adversaries adapt? The co-evolutionary framing maps onto the Failure-First attack-evolution pipeline — the Defender is analogous to a target model being hardened, and the Meta-Attacker is the Failure-First operator generator. Tracking transfer ASR across attack families provides a principled measure of how much safety generalises vs. overfits to the training attack distribution.
