## AI Safety Research Digest — August 2, 2026

> *A date this project flagged as a compliance milestone back in our [July 6 digest](/ai-safety-daily/ai-safety-daily-2026-07-06/) has now arrived: the EU AI Act's transparency rules apply from today, unaffected by the parallel deferral of high-risk obligations.*

### Key Findings

- **Article 50 transparency obligations take effect today.** From 2 August 2026, providers and deployers of certain AI systems in the EU must: inform users when they are interacting with an AI system, clearly mark AI-generated or manipulated content as artificially generated, inform people exposed to emotion-recognition or biometric-categorisation systems that such a system is in use, and disclose deepfakes and AI-generated text as such. See the [European Commission's transparency-obligations guidance](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act) and [artificialintelligenceact.eu's Article 50 text](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/).
- **High-risk obligations are separately deferred — this date does not move them.** The EU's Digital Omnibus on AI is now settled law, not a pending expectation: after a Council–Parliament provisional political agreement reached 6 May 2026 ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)), it was adopted as [Regulation (EU) 2026/1744](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng) of 8 July 2026, published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and in force since 27 July 2026. Under it, high-risk obligations for stand-alone Annex III systems are pushed to 2 December 2027, and for AI embedded in regulated products (Annex I) to 2 August 2028 — see also [Sidley's Data Matters blog](https://datamatters.sidley.com/2026/06/24/eu-ai-act-transparency-obligations-preparing-for-compliance-by-2-august-2026/). One narrower carve-out: watermarking obligations for AI systems already on the market before today are separately delayed to 2 December 2026.

### Implications for Embodied AI

The split we noted in July holds: the *disclosure* obligations that took effect today apply broadly and immediately, while the *safety-case* obligations for physically consequential systems — industrial and logistics robotics, much embodied AI — sit in the deferred Annex I/III categories, with runway now confirmed out to 2027–2028. Article 50's requirements (interaction disclosure, content labeling) are largely orthogonal to physical-harm risk; they do not substitute for the high-risk safety obligations still years away for the systems this project studies. We track this as a governance-timeline data point, not a claim about embodied-AI safety outcomes — no benchmark or measurement in this project speaks to EU regulatory compliance.
